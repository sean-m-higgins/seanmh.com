Film 04 — sunny

Design doc, November 16, 2026. A fourth from life film on version/i-films (worktree wt/i-films), and the first made in Blender. Series plan: films-plan.html.

Goal: a short 3D film about Sunny, Sean's cat: one day in the apartment, from the first patch of morning sun to a nap in the last one. Every beat is a moment that is really in Sean's videos, rebuilt as a small felt-and-card miniature and shot like stop motion.

What is different from films 01–03: those are drawn in JavaScript on a canvas. This one is modelled, lit and animated in Blender 5.3, entirely from Python, headless. Nothing is sculpted or keyframed by hand in the Blender UI, so the film is still code: one command rebuilds every frame.

Status (2026-11-18): steps 1–7 done. The film is rendered: 62.5 s, 1080², 7.5 MiB, with a poster and a 4 s loop, in wt/i-films/public/. It is in the catalog locally. Not committed, not pushed. Sean has not yet watched it: the storyboard and style-frame gates were run straight through, as asked, so his review is the next step.

Sunny, as observed

  • A tripod cat (from Sean, 2026-11-18; the first render missed this and gave him four legs and a long tail). His right hind leg and his tail were amputated shortly before Sean adopted him. He has two front legs, one hind leg and a bob.
  • How he stands: the hind paw comes in under the middle of his body, so the three paws make a triangle. Side-on in the hallway footage the hind leg angles forward under the belly and the rump is high and round.
  • How he moves: he hops. The front legs step in turn; the hind leg stays planted, then the rump lifts, the paw skips forward and the head nods down to take the weight. Faster, it becomes a rocking canter between the front pair and the one hind leg.
  • How he sits: upright, front paws planted wide, the one haunch folded under on his left.
  • A slim, short-haired spotted tabby. Warm sand-brown coat, dark spots on the flanks that join into stripes on the legs, a dark-tipped bob, a tabby “M” on the forehead.
  • Cream chin, muzzle and chest. Brick-pink nose. Large yellow-green eyes, dark-rimmed. Large upright ears.
  • Signature poses: the upright “meerkat” sit; the trot with a toy in the mouth; the vertical leap; the sideways flop; the tight curl in sun.

The film

The feeling. Small, warm, a little funny. Sunny is sharp and wild for ten seconds at a time and then asleep. The film should move the way a day with a cat does: stillness, a burst, stillness.

The anchor. In films 01–03 one drawn element holds a fixed screen position. Here the anchor is a patch of light. Every scene has one (sun through a window, a ceiling spot, a lamp), and Sunny ends each scene in it or leaves it. The film opens on an empty patch of morning sun on the rug and closes on the same framing with Sunny asleep in the evening one. Sunny, in the sun: the name is the film.

The clock. The colour of the light carries the day: pale gold morning, white noon, amber afternoon, blue dusk, a violet-and-red lamp-lit evening, then one last warm patch.

The look

Three candidates, rendered as style frames of the same shot (scene 1):

  • A. Felt miniature (recommended). Sunny is a soft wool-felt toy with needle-felted markings and glass-bead eyes. The rooms are card and paper. Shallow depth of field, like a tabletop photographed up close.
  • B. Papercraft. Everything, Sunny included, is folded card with flat facets (blender-gif).
  • C. Toon. Flat cel colour with an ink edge (closest to blender-short-movie).

Palette. Card cream #f1eadc, wood #d9c3a0, Sunny sand #b8956a, marking brown #4a3828, eye green #b9c545, painting red #d8452e, sock purple #6b3fa0, grey cat #7d7f86. The accent is sunlight itself.

Look pass (2026-11-19): Sean asked whether the graphics could be better. Added: real fur strands on both cats that carry the coat markings, a mouth, wood floors with grain and soft reflections, looped rugs, dust in the air so the hallway lamps and the closing lamp throw visible cones of light, and a compositor bloom with a faint vignette. Fur added almost nothing to render time; the dust in the air roughly doubled it for the three scenes that have it, so the film now takes about 48 minutes to render and is 7.5 MiB.

Three legs (2026-11-18): the model, every pose and all three gaits were rebuilt for a tripod. The model has no right hind leg (the hip is a smooth round), a stronger left hind leg set nearer the midline, and a two-segment bob. The grey cat keeps four legs and a full tail. The gait takes the ground covered per cycle and gives each paw a stance travel in proportion to its own duty, which is what lets the hind leg hop on a different rhythm from the front legs without any paw sliding.

As built (2026-11-18): look A (felt miniature) was chosen; the papercraft frame lost the coat markings and the toon frame lost the sun patch. The three style frames are in incoming/sunny/style-frames/. The last shot ends closer than the opening angle so the sleeping cat and the signature read. Render: about 2 s a frame, 25 minutes for the film. Two renders of the same frame are not bit-identical in EEVEE (at most 2 of 255 levels, in a handful of pixels), so the determinism check is a PSNR comparison, not a pixel hash.

Measured on this machine (2026-11-16): Blender 5.3.0 runs headless with EEVEE on the M1; a 1080² frame of a simple scene takes about 2 s after an 8 s warm-up. Skin modifier plus automatic armature weights work in background mode. At 12 poses a second, a 62 s film is about 745 renders.

Open questions for Sean

None of these block the film; nothing below is shown or stated in it. They matter for the sentence on the wall.

  • Who is the grey cat in the hallway and on the chair? It appears as an unnamed grey cat.
  • Anything to say about Sunny on the page: age, where Sunny came from, how fetch started?
  • The grey bow tie and name tag from the portrait are left out. Say so if Sunny should wear them in the last scene.
  • The red-orange painting over the bed is assumed to be Sean's own and is shown as a simplified red-orange canvas.

Guardrails.

  • The videos never enter git, a deploy, or a frame of the film.
  • No faces and no likeness of a person. Socks and a hand only.
  • No brands, signs or readable text from the backgrounds.
  • Only moments that are in the footage. The day's order is the film's own arrangement.
  • The MP4 stays within 20 MiB. Nothing is pushed without Sean's confirmation.
