Film 05 · purr

A two-minute loop film of the cats from Sean's life — Sunny (the brown tabby tripod), a grey Russian blue, a black cat, and an orange-and-white cat — drawn in code in a warm lofi-illustration style, with an original piano-and-chill-jazz soundtrack, also made in code. It plays on repeat without anyone noticing where it starts.

Status, 2026-11-27: built and release-rendered locally, nothing committed or pushed.
  • public/purr.mp4: 120.000 s, H.264 High 1080², AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo, 12.33 MiB (0.70 Mb/s video + 160 kb/s audio), −18.0 LUFS. Renders at 5.8 frames/s (8 min).
  • Share loop (sunpatch, dissolving into itself): purr-loop.mp4 0.97 MiB, purr-loop.gif 2.17 MiB at 720 px. It is the whole 15 s scene, longer than the brief's 4–8 s.
  • Seam: frame 2879 is 99.7 % of frame 0's morning. The music's wrap jump is inside its normal sample-to-sample range (see src/films/purr/music/README.md).
  • Deterministic: frames 100, 359, 1500, 2160 and 2870 hash identically on the same page and on a fresh page.
  • npm run check (0 errors), npm test (7/7), npm run build, and a headless-Chrome look at /films/ and /films/purr/ all pass.
  • Still to do, with Sean's go-ahead: commit, push version/i-films (a production deploy), wiki/Films.md, and the Nexus moon.
Success means:
  • Played with <video loop>, the last frame flows into the first with no visible jump and the music has no audible seam.
  • Every frame reads as a hand-drawn illustration with depth: ink line, cel shading, warm painted light, layered foreground/midground/background.
  • Four distinct cats, each recognisable by coat; Sunny always has three legs (no right hind) and a bob tail.
  • public/purr.mp4 ≤ 20 MiB including audio; poster and share loop rendered; npm run check, npm test, npm run build pass.

3 · How the loop is seamless

  • Picture. Each scene is drawn from seconds, not 0..1, so it can run before its start. In the last 1.25 s of each scene the next scene fades in, already running (its clock goes from −1.25 s to 0). Scene 8 fades into scene 1 the same way, so frame 2879 is 96 % of morning at −0.04 s and frame 0 is morning at 0 s: the loop point is just another frame of a dissolve.
  • Ambient motion inside a scene (steam, rain, breathing, leaves) uses periods that divide the scene's span, or is continuous through the dissolve, so nothing pops.
  • Music. Exactly 5,760,000 samples at 48 kHz. Everything that rings past bar 32 (piano decay, reverb) is wrapped back onto bar 1, and every LFO's period divides 120 s, so sample N−1 → sample 0 is continuous. The chord at bar 32 turns around into bar 1.
  • Browser. A <video loop> restart can cost a few ms of audio on some browsers. Accepted; the wrap is at a quiet, sustained moment (night → morning).

6 · The look

  • Ink line: warm dark brown (never #000), 2–3 px, round joins; thinner for interior detail.
  • Colour: flat base, one cel-shade step (cooler, a little violet) and a warm highlight step, cut as crescents from each shape against the scene's light direction.
  • Light: each scene has a key light (sun, lamp, grey rain) and its colour grades the whole frame; sun patches carry leaf-dapple. A soft bloom on light sources.
  • Depth: a blurred, desaturated far layer (sky/city/room reflection), a crisp midground (the cat and its set), and an out-of-focus foreground element in most scenes (leaves, a curtain edge).
  • Texture: a faint paper grain over everything, held.

7 · Sound

Made by a separate agent, in parallel, entirely in code (Python + numpy, no installs, original material): a soft felt piano carrying harmony and melody, with a light chill-jazz band (upright-style bass, brushed kit, a warm pad) that follows the day: sparse in the morning, fullest at golden hour, solo piano at night turning back to morning. 64 BPM, 32 bars, exactly 120 s. Target about −18 LUFS, true peak under −1.5 dBTP. Code lives in src/films/purr/music/; output renders/purr/music.wav.

9 · Steps

  1. Plan (this file). Sean asked for it in seanmh.com/.
  2. Start the audio agent with the fixed timing contract (120 s, 64 BPM, 32 bars, scene map). Returns a WAV of exactly 5,760,000 frames with measured seam, loudness and peak.
  3. Kit: ink, cel shading, light, foliage, cached layers, dissolves (src/films/purr/kit.js). Cats (cats.js). A cat sheet still: all four coats and every pose, Sunny with three legs.
  4. Scenes 1–8, one at a time, checking stills at the start, middle and end of each. Contact sheet: does every frame look hand-made? Is there depth? Is the light warm?
  5. Loop check: render the last 2 s + first 2 s as one clip and look at the frames across the wrap. No jump in the picture at frame 2879 → 0.
  6. Release: npm run film:render -- purr --release --audio renders/purr/music.wav, poster, share loop. ≤ 20 MiB, audio stream present, duration 120.000 s, ffprobe clean.
  7. Site: catalog entry, FilmWall sound/loop, check + test + build, a browser look at /films/purr/. Plays with sound, loops, no console errors.
  8. Report to Sean. Nothing committed or pushed until Sean says so (pushing version/i-films deploys).

10 · Gates and guardrails

The film brief asks for Sean's approval at the storyboard and style-frame gates. Sean asked for this film to be completed end-to-end in one go, so this plan stands in for the storyboard, and the style is the one the reference sets. Sean reviews the finished film; any scene can be redrawn after.
