Films — from life

Design plan, September 21, 2026. Temporary feature plan for version/films (created 2026-11-14; worktree wt/films).

Goal: a series of short animated films, drawn by a frontier model the way the Kengo Works films are drawn, but drawn from Sean's own photos. It starts with three:

  1. a coffee table, built by hand (a how-to);
  2. a canvas, built and then painted (a how-to);
  3. the Eagle Scout project, a portable wheelchair ramp, from idea to delivery (a story, not a how-to).

Status: designed, nothing built yet. The first thing to build is the pipeline proof (step 1 below).

Temporary: this file exists only while the work is being done. When it ships, the durable facts go to the wiki (a new Films.md, plus updates to Nexus.md), the open items go to plan.html, and this file is deleted. plan.html stays the only standing plan.

The series

Series title: from life. In drawing, from life means drawn from direct observation. These films are drawn from photos of Sean's life. The work titles are lowercase, following .inspiration's “lowercase titles against sentence-case chrome”. The working titles are:

Credit. Each sentence opens with the model's name, the way Kengo Works does (principle 10). Every film signs itself on its last frame in hand lettering: <title> · claude <model> · <year> (principle 13). The model defaults to Opus 5 and is set per film.

The grammar — what makes three films one series

  • Frame. 1080², 24 fps, drawn on twos: the wob seed changes every second frame, so lines “boil” the way hand-drawn animation does. 45–90 s, silent.
  • The anchor. One element holds a fixed screen position for the whole film, and every cut is a match cut on it, as with answer's dot. This is principle 16's “one thing crosses every seam”, applied over time instead of space.
    • grain: a carpenter's pencil mark on the workpiece.
    • gesso: the canvas rectangle itself.
    • ramp: the threshold line of the door the ramp serves.
  • Two registers, from dew.
    • Bench scenes are warm, top-down and hatched. They show the making.
    • Drawing scenes are navy diagrams. They carry the measurements, the cut list, and the plan or the paperwork.
  • One print technique per film, one shared toolkit.
    • grain: etching and hatching.
    • gesso: risograph halftone with painterly daubs.
    • ramp: a field notebook — pencil, watercolour wash, and tornPath paper scraps. Deckle edges read as memory, as they do in fable.
  • Palette. Eight “pens” per film, on a cream paper ground with an ink that has a hue in it. One accent, rationed (principles 7, 8 and 19).
  • Words. At most 12 real words per film, lowercase, and at most four per caption. The how-to captions are step verbs: measure twice, clamp and wait. Everything else is aword scribble (principle 15). Dimensions are allowed as drawn callouts.
  • Shape. The last frame rhymes with the first. Each film is built backwards from its final image (principle 14).
  • Film and page split the job. The film carries the sequence and the feeling. The page carries the recipe: materials, tools and steps, with each step linked to its moment in the film.

The three films

Beat lists are drafts, and every beat is a single scene. A fact marked (confirm) must come from Sean before it is drawn — nothing is invented. That rule is carried over from h-gaudi's docs/photographs.md.

01 · grain — coffee table (how-to, about 50 s, etching)
  1. Blank sheet. One pencil stroke lays down the anchor mark.
  2. Drawing register: the dimensioned sketch and the cut list (confirm dimensions).
  3. Rough boards laid out, top-down (confirm species; Scroll says oak, h-gaudi avoids naming it).
  4. Measure and mark — measure twice.
  5. Cut.
  6. Joinery (confirm method).
  7. Dry fit.
  8. Glue and clamp — clamp and wait. The light swings across the bench to show time passing.
  9. Sand.
  10. Finish. Colour enters the hatching for the first time.
  11. The slatted lower shelf, then the glass set into the top.
  12. The table in its room, feat. Sunny. It closes on the pencil mark, now under the finish. Signature.
02 · gesso — canvas and painting (how-to, about 50 s, risograph)
  1. The canvas rectangle is fixed on screen for the whole film.
  2. Stretcher bars cut and squared (drawing register: corner angles, size (confirm)).
  3. Fabric pulled taut and stapled, the staples landing in rhythm around the frame.
  4. Gesso, coat by coat, drying between.
  5. Sketch.
  6. Paint layers build up.
  7. The finished painting — one of Sean's (flower, orange, red or space) (confirm which), redrawn in halftone.
  8. Hung on a wall. Signature.
03 · ramp — Eagle Scout project (story, up to about 90 s, field notebook)
  1. The problem: steps up to a door, and a wheelchair at the bottom. The threshold is the anchor.
  2. The idea, as a notebook sketch.
  3. The plan (drawing register): the rise and the slope. The ADA standard is 1:12 (confirm the actual figures).
  4. Approvals and fundraising: stamps, signatures, scribbled paperwork (confirm what happened).
  5. Build days: many hands (confirm who helped, and the hours).
  6. The portable beat: it folds, or loads into a vehicle (confirm how).
  7. Delivery (confirm to whom and when).
  8. The first roll across the threshold.
  9. The opening door again, now with a ramp. Signature.

Pipeline — photos to film

  1. Intake. Photos go in gitignored incoming/<slug>/ on the films branch. A script sorts them by their EXIF capture time and writes reference copies no larger than 1568 px for the model to read. It reuses the orientation and resize handling from h-gaudi's scripts/prepare-woodworking.mjs, minus the WebP output. Nothing leaves incoming/.
  2. Photo log and facts. The model looks at each photo and records the step and what it shows. Sean confirms every fact.
  3. Storyboard. Scenes, each with its duration, register, caption, anchor position and source photo filenames. The storyboard stays local, and Sean approves it before anything is drawn.
  4. Style frames. Three finished stills drawn with the toolkit. Sean approves the look.
  5. Film module. One deterministic module per film. Time comes from the frame index, never from requestAnimationFrame — an occluded or headless Chrome throttles it to nothing. 
    // src/films/grain/film.js
export default {
  slug: "grain", title: "grain", model: "Opus 5", year: 2026,
  fps: 24, size: 1080, seed: 190926,
  scenes: [
    { id: "sketch", dur: 4.0, caption: null, step: "Draw it and cut list",
      ref: ["IMG_2041.jpg"],                 // filenames only; photos stay in incoming/
      draw(ctx, t, R) { /* t: 0..1 through the scene, R: seeded, reseeded on twos */ } },
    // …
  ],
};
  6. Preview. A local-only page with a scrubber, a ?t= parameter and a frame counter.
  7. Render. scripts/render-film.mjs <slug>. It uses Node 22 and headless Chrome over CDP, with no dependencies — the pattern already used for the kengoworks survey. It sets an exact 1080² viewport with Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride, then renders each frame by index and pipes the PNGs into ffmpeg: 
    ffmpeg -f image2pipe -framerate 24 -i - \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slow -tune animation -crf 20 \
  -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart public/grain.mp4
    The same run writes the poster (WebP) and a WebVTT chapters track generated from the scenes that have a step.
  8. Budget. Each MP4 must be 20 MiB or less (Pages rejects files over 25 MiB), and the render script enforces it. Only release renders are committed; drafts stay in gitignored renders/. If a film cannot fit at acceptable quality, the fallback is an R2 bucket behind the Worker. That is a decision to make once the pipeline proof has measured the real bitrate, not a default.

  • Moons.
    • One glass bead per child, using the orb's fresnel shell shader at a radius of about 0.2.
    • The beads orbit on a ring about 1.75 across, tilted about 18° below the orb's equator, and they follow the orb's scale.
    • They are always visible, so the count reads from across the ring.
    • They respond only while Films is the active, settled orb: activePortalAt also tests that orb's moons.
    • Hovering a moon pauses its orbit, brightens it, and sets the label to that film.
    • Clicking a moon flies into it and opens /films/<slug>/. That needs beginEnter to take a target position and an href. Clicking the orb opens /films/.
  • Without a mouse.
    • The active label lists the films as real links. That is the main path for keyboard and phone users, since a moon is too small to rely on as a tap target.
    • ArrowUp and ArrowDown move the moon highlight.
    • Both fallbacks (the no-WebGL list and the <noscript> list) nest the film links under Films.
    • With reduced motion, the moons hold still and entering uses the veil fade.
  • Back-button fix, which comes first. Nothing resets entering or the veil. A visitor who returns with Back from the back/forward cache is left facing the veil colour. Films make that round trip the normal case, so add a pageshow handler that resets the entry state, the veil and the camera when event.persisted is true.

What Sean provides, per film

  • Photos. 15–40, in order, covering every step: materials laid out, measurement close-ups, joints and details, and the finished piece from three angles and in place. Top-down and consistent angles translate best. For ramp, old phone photos and pictures of the paperwork both count.
  • A facts sheet.
    • grain: wood species, dimensions, joinery, finish, time and cost, tools.
    • gesso: canvas size, materials, and which painting.
    • ramp: year, who it was for, troop, hours, helpers, funding, how it is portable, rise and slope.
  • The model to credit. Opus 5 unless Sean says otherwise.

Guardrails.

  • No link to /films/ is published before seanmh-films is live.
