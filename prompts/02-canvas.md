## The feeling

A new apartment, high up, with white walls everywhere and no colour. Making
something big with almost no tools, on the floor among your own furniture. Two
moods, one film: the careful, square, practical **build** (hand saw, staples,
rotate, staple) and then the **release** of painting, where anything goes
because art is free. The film should move from tight and measured to loose and
joyful, and end with colour on a white wall.

## Facts (from Sean)

- Materials from Home Depot / Lowe's, art stores like Blick, and fabric
  stores. (Draw generic shops, no branding.)
- A small apartment without most power tools: hand saws, sanders, a drill, a
  staple gun.
- A canvas can be any size, and can be **custom shapes beyond rectangles**.
- Two phases: build the frame and canvas, then paint.
- Built for himself, at a fraction of the cost of buying a canvas or artwork
  that size.

## The story (Sean's words, lightly edited; keep its substance)

This begins with a need to decorate, in my case a new apartment with white
walls everywhere and no colour. Find the wall and picture a new piece that will
fit it perfectly. Then decide its size and an early design. It doesn't have to
be fully formed yet.

Write down everything you need before going to the store. The basics: wood for
the frame (exact measurements), screws and hardware for the frame, staples,
fabric, paint and brushes. Gather it all and bring it home. Then the building
begins.

Start by cutting the wood with a hand saw to the exact sizes, sanding the
edges if needed. Assemble the frame to the design, usually a rectangle around
the outside, with extra support pieces across larger frames so the wood
doesn't flex. With the frame built, lay the fabric out, set the frame on it,
and stretch the fabric around to the back. Staple and rotate, staple and
rotate, until there are staples all the way around. To finish this part, trim
the excess fabric on the back. The canvas is built.

Next comes the painting. Prime the canvas first. Without primer, the fabric
soaks up the oil. With a clean white slate, the art can begin, from whatever
ideas come to mind: inspiration from an art museum, from something online, or
from life itself. I always find it easier once I begin, even when the idea
isn't fully formed. Paint with oils (or whatever paint you prefer), use a
medium so it spreads more easily, mix colours, use texture, chase new ideas.
Anything goes, because art is free. At the end, let the paint dry. And as the
final step, hang it on the wall and enjoy!

## The look (choose at the style-frames gate)

Render one style frame of each and let Sean pick:

- **A. Line, then pastel** (recommended). The build is in clean, sparse
  pencil and ink on cream; at beat 10 the whole film floods into dense chalk
  and pastel (`chalk1-3`). The style change is the story: structure, then
  freedom.
- **B. Risograph** (films-plan's choice). Cream paper, two or three spot inks,
  halftone dots, misregistration; painterly daubs for the painting.
- **C. Cut paper** (`layered1-4`). The frame and fabric as layered paper with
  cast shadows; paint as torn coloured paper.

**Palette seed:** wall white with a warm cast `#f4f1ea`, pencil `#33324a`,
pine `#e6cf9f`, raw canvas `#e9e0cc`, then for the painting the colours of
Sean's actual piece, sampled from IMG_1584 (sky blue, sunflower yellow, poppy
red, leaf green) or IMG_5102 (black, violet, glowing yellow-green); accent:
the first stroke of paint.
